Temag 2025 Trip Report
======================

Day 1: Drive Up. 18 km, 2 portages (0.4 km)
-------------------------------------------

Depart Waterloo @ 6:30 AM with two canoes on the Patte wagon. Lance drove on ahead to pick up Malcolm and Will in Trout Creek. We all met up at Dan’s Place in Sturgeon Falls for lunch/brunch. Then 2.5 hour drive to Wawiagama Access point. Road just got worse and worse until it was truly questionable if we were gonna have to start the trip with a 10 km portage. But the Patte mobile made it “just fine:”

On the water by 3 pm, Easy 4 km paddle down Wawiagama Lake. Issue with a wrong campsite location on Adventure maps caused me to take the crew down the wrong creek (which was flowing into Wawiagama not out of it, which I was sure it should do… but we remedied the situation and found Wawiagama Creek. Creek wasn’t too bad. Twisty and some fallen log/beaver dam liftovers but reasonably navigable:  
![Wawiagama Creek](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczMBrJ5sfmXYrMXC93hsT8ecR8XQZ-XEE4QIH_5YVJn3VCBppLMsjqkxm2SnxCu860CyhXRU6WhySun6mXT6KJN2muG016kOe_tsC_ZDiFHkBvMZGKBEBHxl1ftF7oh3dBsOOCASJ4b85KwOp9iq2mTbHQ=w985-h1313-s-no-gm?authuser=0)  
Definitely “high for August” water levels. Wawiagama emptied into Obabika River, 10 km later and few more liftovers and we were at Obabika Falls (where Obabika River flows into Sturgeon). It was 7 pm by then and decided to camp at the somewhat janky portage campsite (Campsite Tier: D) as there were no mapped campsites for a while after that and we had a big dinner of steak and potatoes to cook and consume:  
![First Campsite](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNZFMhv5f-HQYwOS7Rzu5xYfJN3yD-rOIsENfBQGKqnQloDQVwmAGKSHB3uONM3HmQ3TO9xL5roQtppQIcIROqTL09CE4cVHEdEXowYq3Cp0Hug747kENsjd72oqrXrlgK-oxEw4udqMxkCvl52Tbkzqg=w1751-h1313-s-no-gm?authuser=0)  
Bedtime routine of Malcolm/Will “shutting things down for the night” while Luke, Mike, me and Lance hit the hay earlier was nicely established.

Day 2: Sturgeon River + Kelly Bypass: 45 km, 5 portages (2 km)
--------------------------------------------------------------

The morning routine of 6 am wakeup, Luke fetching water and firewood, me getting a fire going, and Mike+Lance getting a start on breakfast prep established. Malcolm and Will would sleep in till the coffee was ready, then help w breakfast cooking/dishes. We’d make ~3 L of boiling water for coffee on a super small open fire then usually cook breakfast using a 2.5 L pot and a frying pan, sometimes using the biolite twig stove if needed. Most campsites didn’t have a grill so every firepit I’d build a little stack of rocks to balance a pot/fry pan across.

Breakfast today was leftover potatoes, real eggs, pre cooked bacon, and sourdough bread. On the water by ~8:30 am after a ~800 m portage to start to the bottom of Obabika Falls. Sturgeon River was very sandy, like clean beach sand and current was pretty good (put in a 9 km in one hour here). Portage around Lower Goose Falls had a bit of a sketchy put in closer to the lip of the waterfall than I’d have liked but was fine:  
![](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczPKBqu1Z4BiFz9d9-2sJX8YCbGamM7XPoVhD6lnwGpiilxwxwDSrVa_rB9ROOZuhLG6dz2Tx4LRA6uhKSTwuSNjneyk7NlT92RgAg0qUp9NbonxbAzGHSro1u2PqCQschuT-SSAVZG0Pfvz4UVlmgLu4Q=w1751-h1313-s-no-gm?authuser=0)  
Temps climbed and climbed but fortunately river water, being mostly groundwater driven this time of year and having a consistent current and occasional small rapids/swifts (so good circulation/no stratification), was super cold/refreshing. We had lunch of hummus, naan, green peppers, and refried beans on a beauty beach on an inside bend of the river:  
![](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczNG6gq8SlxbCBI_ltuXQ-lFW5f38vQn_WezGXA4tIhY8QFCyeckx8Il3WYTjXFSpXqeJ0IDBAWK5vIolidMPCllzJBYOwcFuOAoeWGu9R8w66RAxbFFUXir7WokHjgIWcT-xM4ylT_pM9IjU-J4_-EaHA=w1751-h1313-s-no-gm?authuser=0)  
Lance filled up bottles (he was the king of drinking water on the trip, always had his gravity filter set up for us). After about 36 km of paddling we reached Kelly’s Portage, where we ran into a group of girls from Camp Wabun. They looked pretty heat-cooked/dejected and were about to start a monster 3.5 km portage when we found them. Their counsellors (probably like 17 years old) asked us if we had any advice on the bypass (which we were taking, it seems to be less used, --> involves three 300ish m portages and about 8 km of paddling across some smaller lakes/ponds to reach Maskinonge Lake from the Sturgeon River. I didn’t have any real advice to give as none of us had been there before and we too had no idea what to expect. I’m not sure what they ended up deciding doing, but their goal was Wolf Lake three days/two nights from then. If they made it through that section I imagine the rest was pretty easy. We completed a series of pretty overgrown but totally passable portages and paddled through shallow but also totally navigable sections on the bypass:  
![](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczMBp8Kxq1kEd7Jme6-TrQCOVVzi3TxHAUa3wt3Ip6IS8qM4wDjveOD6WRSnInt9xS7GuaAOavhj4t5fu3EptbPmvqezqFY_vPYFo7Rais1w89S-ifvH9D8Eq1-qL3nJ3twZrWLh2WCFpWI_tmZqBc_3vA=w1751-h1313-s-no-gm?authuser=0)  
Reached a nice island site (B + Tier, would have been A tier but it was lacking a thunderbox) on Maskinonge lake by 4:30 pm, having put in a massive distance 45 km day which seems crazy but actually not super hard as very little portage and we were current-assisted for the vast majority of the paddle. Dinner of sausages and buns cooked on sticks over the fire, and a nice sunset: ![](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczPRXXKO5o-Q_L-i8Yy271R0EHbOGZfI8MyV_W_a28vmt3ZNEFIu3dXFO4QgkBYUazI2DG52pOUZx6xwFl5luysHBF4XyHoyInhJsZPeIpkUDbhU5qHDDEUDFYEOgMd2ONCBxfqnn6HlpaS_bkq62w-usg=w1751-h1313-s-no-gm?authuser=0)

Breakfast of Oatmeal w maple syrup and “magic nuts” (super gourment honey glazed pecans, cashews etc.). Broke camp by 8:30. Easy paddle heading north up Maskinonge Lake (only legit tailwind of the trip), but the building wind was actually foreboding as we were gradually making a 180 degree turn today to head south down the Matagasmi lake after a series of short portages up a few nice waterfalls. Sure enough, when we hit McCarthy Bay it was nasty head/crosswind and big whitecaps. We fought our way to a campsite and stopped for lunch:  
![](https://lh3.googleusercontent.com/pw/AP1GczPOmABXD5Kky9BfGXpVXSY_Rse5jjOe63e_j80VM_Nh_LLKT5S4DmlVP8RMF8pIExO8hePa5k7iN7TZ4bURRZDTaA2B_YwMwcWkhoIW6AJyD1H5NNRv5KFPm9f_wN1FX4ygKfqCgiNJt1X_Bewkrx9W5A=w1751-h1313-s-no-gm?authuser=0)  
Another tough ~8 km/90 min grind into the wind heading south on Matagasmi and we made it around the horn to head northwest towards Wolf Lake. Two easy but uphill portages to Wolf Lake, which we paddled around a bit looking for best campsite at around 4:30 pm. Was kinda busy (maybe 3-4 other groups staying on this lake as well) Ended up scoring the “mine shaft” site on top of a small cliff looking at Wolf Mountain (easy A Tier, possibly S if swimming were easier to access). Dinner of mushroom risotto and smoked salmon. Another beaut of a sunset and swim before ~8:30 bedtime:

I got to the fork in the road, dropped the canoe, and jogged the 100 m to check out the first rapid (which you can see from the portage put in). On K-Cal’s map in hindsight it shows the two sets as class II and I respectively. The 700 m portage to Cat’s Lake, marked as one portage on Jeff’s Map, is actually two portages, like 100 m, a tiny beaver pond hop, then 600 m. The portage from Witches Lake to Yorston Lake was kinda tricky to navigate because of ATV trails/logging roads, and the non-direct/unintuitive direction of the trail. Yorston was a nice 6 km paddle, only interrupted by me going on shore at a recently vacated campsite to extinguish someone’s fire for them. Seriously, you could have dumped pine needles on it and it would have ignited. Pissed me right off that someone would leave a campfire like that, despite the ominous smokey haze blanketing us. Put me in a bad mood as it was a rude middle of trip reminder that people can really really suck. Overall
-------

A great trip and longest one in Temagami to date, ~208 km, with roughly 22.5 km of it carrying over 55 portages. Definitely harder/more “in perpetual motion” than the last two trips, which had easy days/multiple days at same campsite midway so we could knock off maple mountain/ishpatina ridge. For me, what made this trip special was the variety of terrain: the contrast between the sandy/twisty sturgeon river and the typical rugged/rocky “classic” temagami in the upland areas was absolutely crazy, and only served to reinforce what we already knew: Temagami truly represents the mecca of canoe tripping.

The crew worked pretty well together. Around camp I did most of the cooking/fire building with Malcolm and Lance helping make their “specialty” meals (steak and potatoes+annies mac for Malcolm, pancakes for Lance), everyone else did dishes/firewood gathering etc. I definitely made strides in the stern paddling department being able to J-stroke in rhythm with Lance’s power stroke in the bow: this made us significantly faster in a straight line than in past years (7 km/h on flat water was suddenly an achievable thing). Both Lance’s and Luke’s bow paddling prowess definitely took a big step forward (rock avoidance, quick bow draws to help spin the boat on twisty stuff, knowing when to dismount in shallows etc.).

Weather didn’t leave much to complain about: super hot to start, one decent but short thunderstorm midway, then sunny/smokey after that, only one bad case/tough stretch of wind. We ate really really well, which to me is worth travelling super heavy the first few days for. The mosquitoes and even blackflies were absolutely terrible on some of the portages (worst I’ve ever experienced in Temagami, especially in August), but our campsites were pretty good on that front. Blueberries and wintergreen (spearmint-flavoured leaves) were plentiful throughout.

Already thinking about next years trip :-D
