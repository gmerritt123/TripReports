La Cloche 2025 Trip Report

Day 2: Murray - Nellie: 7 km, 1 portage (1.4 km portaging)
----------------------------------------------------------

Rain started about 5 am and didn’t let up till 9 or so, and since we didn’t have far to go at all today we slept in till 830-9. Breakfast of coffee, real eggs, bacon and bread. Slowly broke camp and paddled over to the Notch Portage, which we two-tripped because we basically had all day to do it and it’s a nice walk along a creek that runs down from Nellie to Murray. Nellie is pretty well known as one of Killarney’s famous “acid” lakes: lakes whose inflow is dominated by direct precipitation and direct runoff (off predominately exposed quartzite), which gives it extremely limited buffering capacity. Their natural acidity was “enhanced” by acid rain from nearby historic mining operations, which effectively ended all life in the lake… which results in spookily crystal clear water… making the lake now a highly sought after backcountry camping destination to “enjoy nature in” (sigh).

It rained a bit more in the morning, cleared up by afternoon, but was actually super cold as it got really really windy (like 70 km/h gusts), which put our tent pegging/tarp rigging to the test. We bushwacked/followed a few faint footpaths and gathered a tonne of firewood (not only for us, but also for whoever was there after us as there was zero firewood there when we arrived). Overall a pretty relaxed and lazy day reading by the water. Dinner of risotto and smoked salmon and bed by 7:30 pm as it started to rain heavily and the temp dropped, and we had nothing else to do other than get warm and read in our sleeping bags.

Day 5: Wright’s Lake to Fort La Cloche (22 km hike)
---------------------------------------------------

Out of our warm sleeping bags at 6 am, coffee and instant oatmeal and broke camp. We figured we had only 16 km left, and based on our previous rate, we’d be done around noon. That turned out to be a pretty bad estimation for a number of reasons because of what lay ahead of us:

1.  The most rugged section of trail;
2.  The least well marked section of trail;
3.  The gpx file we were semi-relying on definitely either went off track in places or the data recording itself was just plain wrong.
4.  There was ~22 km of hiking left, not 16 km

The ruggedness of the trail made it hard to intuitively stay on it, so we’d try to follow trail markers - these were usually white markers nailed on trees, but also cairns along the ridges and flagging tape at times. But oftentimes there was conflict between these markers: sometimes the flagging tape would take you down a dead end side trails and/or alternate routes, or other times it would help link you between two far apart cairns or white trail markers. Making it even more conflicting was the gps file we had, which sometimes deviated significantly from the trail, but oftentimes also helped us know what direction the trail was supposed to be when we clearly went off it. So ultimately there was no singular source of “truth” for us to follow and lots of conflicting information. At one point there were literally two white trail markers clearly directing a left and right turn simultaneously, and lots of visible trail markers in either direction. We chose one of them, and sure enough it petered out and we found ourselves seriously off trail. But we kept on finding flagging tape, and the gps file wasn’t deviating signficantly from our route, so we just kept going. Then we really found ourself bushwacking and realized the gps file had to be way off, and we weren’t really sure of the best way to get back to the trail because of a) the terrain, and b) we didn’t really know where the trail was anymore. But we kinda stuck with the bushwacking, criss crossing the gps track while trying to follow the easiest terrain, and eventually, eventually, linked back up with a clearly defined trail. Suffice to say this slowed us down a lot.

Overall
-------

Overall a very fun and interesting way to string together two trips in one, and obviate the need for a shuttle between the east/west trailheads of heaven’s gate. We had a massive first day paddle of ~46 km, two super chill days on Nellie, then two really big days making our way back to Fort La Cloche via Heaven’s Gate. The weather was marginal for early September: rained on and off every day except the last day, lows around 5-6 degrees, highs of 13-15, and crazy windy for most of it. That said, we never actually got wet once - either the rain was brief enough to not really break through the forest canopy, or camp was already made and we were comfortable under the tarp. Bringing both a saw and hatchet also really helped for gathering dry firewood. And we both had the right gear to stay warm at night. And with those temps I think I saw a grand total of 5 mosquitoes the entire time.

The Heaven’s Gate trail exceeded expectations in terms of both spectacular views/beauty and difficulty. I am really glad we pushed far on day one of the hike because that second half was an absolute doozy: if doing Heaven’s Gate do not take it lightly and give lots of extra time for getting lost/off trail. There’s almost no “auto-pilot” type hiking at all. But the rewards are pretty special. That green valley between the La Cloche ridges was something else.

The overall numbers, 70 km paddled, 5 km portaged (with ~200 m elevation), 43 km hiked (with ~2300 m elevation).
