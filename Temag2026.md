Day 2: Mendelsohn Lake to Tupper Lake, 24 km, 15 portages (5.6 km worth), ~7.5 hours
-------------------------------------------

Solid rain overnight but woke up to clearing skies. Breakfast of coffee, real eggs and sourdough bread. On the water around 9 am. Day started with a gruelling 2 km portage from Mendelsohn to Skull Lake, which was largely destroyed by a 2018 forest fire and hasn't seen much maintenance since. Tonnes of fallen trees and overgrowth make it almost a full bushwack at times. We had done this one before at least so knew what we were getting into. After Skull Lake we headed south via the "overland" route, thinking the creek route option would be jammed full of deadfall. The overland route is essentially hops/skips over a collection of small ponds and a bunch of short portages also destroyed by the 2018 fire. A lot of loading/unloading and bushwacking. We made our way through that section and reached Anvil Lake, where we stopped for a quick lunch and decided to push on to Tupper Lake at the foot of Maple Mountain, which would mean we wouldn't have to break camp the next day and all we'd have planned is a short paddle across Tupper to summit Maple Mountain. We reached the same campsite we stayed on in years prior, which gets a solid A- tier rating from me: limited tent space, half decent swimming, amazing firepit/sitting area and view of Maple Mountain.

We reached Katherine Lake (formerly Dividing Lake, where the Trout Streams splits off the main channel of the Lady Evelyn River). Having ascended the Trout Streams, we made a "left" to head down the Lady Evelyn, a few portages (including the one past Cabin Falls where Hap Wilson's ecolodge is on the other side of the river), to reach the bonafide S Tier Bridal Veil Campsite around 1-2 pm. Day 5: Bridal Veil Falls to South End of Lady Evelyn Lake, 20 km, 7 portages (4 km worth), ~5.5 hours

Breakfast of coffee, oatmeal, maple syrup, blueberries, and honey roasted nuts. After descending the Lady Evelyn, we reached the "two miler" portage, a 3.2 km portage I had done before - long but not technical. After powering through it we ran into some Park rangers/wardens on the other side, who were doing portage maintenance. There was some trepidation about the upcoming 3.2 km portage, which we had on the docket a few days from now, as we knew it was in the same burn area as the Mendelsohn-Skull portage and could potentially be in even worse shape. So I asked the rangers if they knew anything about the state of it and they told me they cut it about two weeks ago and they almost ran out of chainsaw gas doing it. Fantastic news, because it meant that portage would likely be in relatively great shape. After the portage we loaded up the canoes and paddled the length of Diamond Lake and headed north across the Lady Evelyn Falls portage, arriving at an absolutely massive S Tier campsite on a long point on the south shore of Lady Evelyn. Matt and I camped out right out on the point with an amazing view of the stars.

Day 6: South End of Lady Evelyn Lake to Shangri La Campsite on Lady Evelyn River, 28 km, 10 portages (2.7 km worth), ~6 hours 
-------------------------------------------

Up well before dawn to make breakfast of coffee, hash browns, rehydrated eggs and summer sausage. On the water by 7 am, amazingly efficient camp break. Started raining about 2 hours into our travel day but was super warm with no wind, no raincoats necessary. The day started with ~17 km of paddle, across Lady Evelyn Lake, Willow Island, and Sucker Gut Lakes, with only two short portages. I got a nice shift in paddling with Patte here. Then back up the Trout Streams portages to Katherine Lake. The portages up this time were notably more treacherous because it was wet now. Had another short snack/lunch break at Centre Falls, then we worked out way up the rest of the portages, reaching Katherine Lake by ~noon. At far end of Katherine Lake we pulled over to chat with Hap Wilson, who was waiting for float plane to pickup Ecolodge guests. The intel gathered from Hap was that the alternative to the 3.2 km portage to Makobe i.e. graymud lake would be muddy and brutal this time of year, and that the Makobe River would be very bony and we'd be walking canoes down it. So with that info we resolved that the next day we'd definitely do the 3.2 km portage from Gray's River over to Makobe Lake, as the Park Rangers had confirmed it was cleared, and the alternative sounded like a potential mud disaster. After a quick chat with Hap we paddled onto the Shangri La campsite which was legitimately A, maybe S Tier on the side of a beautiful rapid, but the weather turned much wetter so we scrambled to set up the tarp, gather dry firewood and get tents set up.

Day 7: Shangri La Campsite on Lady Evelyn River to Fifth Falls, 37 km, 12 portages (5.8 km worth), ~9.5 hours
-------------------------------------------

Lots of rain overnight but woke to nice clear skies. Lance churned out the pancakes and I got the coffee going while the other guys started shaking out and packing up wet tents. We paddled about 4 km up the Lady Evelyn River (mostly Macpherson Lake at this point) and made a right at the fork to start ascending Gray's River. There were a few easy portages and beaver dams but not too hard and we made good progress, arriving at the 3.2 km portage in well under 2 hours. At this point we had definitely entered "the burn" i.e. the area destroyed by a 2018 forest fire but we could also see fresh cuts on fallen trees from the park rangers 2 weeks ago. 

Now usually a 3+ km portage is not a "highlight" of the trip but this one definitely was. The trail/portage itself was super easy to follow, even the rerouted sections: the rangers did an amazing job cutting/clearing it, and the portage itself was relatively flat, and our food barrels were light at this point, so it wasn't hard to make progress at all. The landscape was surreal/otherworldly: it kinda felt like the tundra with tiny little new stunted spruce trees popping up, but also so many dead burned/charred trees, on the high points of the portage the sightlines were huge because of a total lack of canopy, and it just seemed like this "new regrowth post hellscape" went on forever. The other thing about lack of canopy: blueberries. So. Many. Blueberries. I've been to Maple Moutain at peak: that was a puddle of blueberries, this was an ocean. It was literally 3 km of the densest blueberry fields any of us had ever seen. At any point on the portage you could have dropped what you were carrying and picked enough blueberries to make multiple pies without having to physically move more than 6 feet. Around the halfway mark I stopped and put the canoe down to wait for Matt and Greg (make sure they were on trail etc.) and in the < 5 mins I was waiting I must have eaten a pint of blueberries without moving an inch from the rock I was sitting on. It was absolutely absurd, and I'm not sure I'll ever see anything like it again.

We reached Makobe lake at the end of the portage and it was absolutely gorgeous: it's a big lake and very remote and for the first time on the trip really I felt we were super super "out there" in parts unknown (to me at least, this trip had a lot of overlap with past trips, showing Matt/Greg the big highlights like Maple Mtn, Centre/Bridal Veil Falls etc.). The right side (east side) of the lake had been burned out of the fire but the left hadn't. We stopped for lunch on a decent campsite, went for a swim and Lance filled the bottles. Then a big open water crossing across the main body of the lake (thankfully wind/water was super calm here) and a short portage to Banks Lake, which was a little more burned out (a number of destroyed campsites), but also beautiful and remote-feeling. Then we reached the head of the Makobe River and began our descent. Patte more or less took the lead on deciding what portages were skippable vs not (he's definitely the most competent whitewater guy in our group) but we quickly realized following him wasn't always the best option because a) we were 3 people in the canoe (with 2 of them 200+ lbs) meaning we hit rocks a lot more often and a lot harder than he did, and b) his canoe was already half-falling apart and he was largely at the point of not caring about its condition anymore. So that meant Jasper Matt and I had to largely figure out for ourselves: do we a) run the rapid with all 3 of us, b) disembark Matt to reduce draft, c) disembark me/Jasper to reduce draft more, d) disembark everyone and walk the canoe down, or e) go full discharge and carry the canoe. It was always improvised and we definitely didn't always choose the optimal option. Lance had a bit more leeway as he had a narrower canoe and way less draft than us so was able to get through rapids we couldn't. We kept pushing on until reaching 5th falls campsite after a very long 9.5 hour day. 

I give the 5th falls campsite a solid A tier: good firepit, good thunderbox, amazing rapid view/white noise machine with good swimming, the only things keeping it from S tier was a lack of amazing tent spots and no obvious/easy jump/dive off swim spots (good swimming at the base of the rapid, just shallow).

<img src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/c2c79993-5f66-4125-8a1c-58afe7459a57" alt="image" style="width:100%; !important; height:auto !important;;">

We set up camp quickly and heated up a big pot of chile with sourdough bread, which had actually kept wonderfully well on the trip (doubled the salt content when I baked them). At this point we discussed tomorrow's plan as well: since we had pushed an extra ~8-9 km today, tomorrow would only be ~20 km and then final day about 23 km. There were only two potential campsites according to our map: we decided we'd stay if the campsites were good, otherwise we'd put in a mega day and get back to the cars. Had a beauty sleep under the stars.

Day 8 Fifth Falls to Mountain Chutes, 43 km, "11" portages (2.9 km worth), ~9.5 hours
-------------------------------------------

Woke and made coffee + oatmeal. Beaut Sunrise looking downriver.

<img src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/9ebe1e05-af89-46c9-b433-18c7a9d72faa" alt="image" style="width:100%; !important; height:auto !important;;">

Efficiently broke camp and continued our way down the Makobe River. On paper we had 11 portages but we knew if it was anything like yesterday we'd be "half skipping" them by walking canoe down, running them, or some hybrid version. Our canoes took a pretty big beating scraping on rocks but only Patte's seemed to be really taking structural damage: my epoxy/gelcoat was scratched to absolute hell but by the end of the trip I only found kevlar exposed in 4 spots: both ends near the keel, on solid chip on the bottom, and another really high scrape/gouge near the gunnel that I can distinctly remember causing. Patte's boat on the other hand needed some gorilla tape as a few of his DIY fibreglass patches were chipping/flaking off and he was taking on water as a result. Lance's boat seemed to be taking the least beating, simply due to having way, way less draft than our boats. We made our way down river and arrived at the two potential campsites shortly after noon: the first one was C tier, affected by the 2018 forest fire, and the second one was effectively non-existent. So that sealed our fate of doing a massive second day in a row and pushing back to the cars at Mountain Chutes. We skipped/waded the rapid the second campsite was supposed to be, then went into the second set, supposed to be ~600 m.

Now here's where things got interesting: Lance immediately went to the portage to carry his canoe through. The rest of us tried making our way down the rapid but realized the best course of action was to have Matt and Jasper get out of my canoe and have me run the rapid with all the gear, and have Greg get out of Patte's canoe and have Patte run it solo as well. Greg for some reason (probably was some legitimate logic at the time) took out his big canoe pack as well, probably to lessen the load in the canoe. So we had Jasper, Matt and Greg bushwack over to the portage trail (intercepting Lance there), while Patte made his way down the rapid with me following his "expert" lines. Patte and I got to the bottom of the rapid and saw zero sign of a portage exit/put in, and it had seemed like the rapid went on a lot longer than 600 m. And there was no way we were pulling the canoes back up the rapid. So I started walking up the rapid, at times swimming, other times hopping along the dry rocks on the river's edge, until I saw Lance back in the solo boat rolling downstream. I yelled to him if Jasper et al were up there and he yelled back that they were and that they were doubling back on the portage trail thinking were were above them having had some sort of boat issue in the rapid. Oh boy, okay. It turned out the rapid did indeed almost go twice as far as the portage trail, so I walked/waded/swam upstream about 600 m, at which point I saw Greg's big pack at the portage exit, which there was no way we could have seen mid rapid on our way down. I waited there a few mins and Greg emerged from the trees, saying Jasper and Matt were still back there on the portage. I told Greg the situation and sent him on his way downstream, having to float his big pack at times/use it as a floaty balance point. The rocks underwater were outrageously slippery, it was actually safer/better to be either in waist deep water (utilizing personal buoyancy to slip/fall less) or to be on shore on grippier dry rocks entirely out of the water, but never be in between (i.e. never be in ankle/knee deep water) as that was prime slip and fall and break a bone territory. Anyway, a fews mins after sending Greg downstream Jasper and Matt emerged from the portage and we all started downstream, catching up to Greg pretty fast because we were largely unencumbered compared to him having to deal with a full 115 L pack. I helped Greg with the pack and we all made it back to the boats. Whew. I guess the lesson learned is you can't count on the portage ending where the rapid ends sometimes and when you have trail walkers out of sight of rapid-runners you really gotta be mindful of that fact. It was absolutely noone's fault we got separated, we were all operating on reasonable logic and all of that logic was predicated on making sure noone was left behind. 

We lost maybe 30-45 mins of time getting through this rigamarole but got rolling again and made it to the 500 m portage into Elk Lake, which is more or less an ATV trail through a trailer park. We stopped for a "final lunch", shovelling in the last of the wraps, salami, pepperettes and trail mix. Then back into the boats for a final ~18 km paddle from Elk Lake down the Montreal River to Mountain Chutes. The final paddle was definitely a slog: there was a decent southwest wind, so head-cross coming from our right, and the Montreal was more a really long lake through this part. We kinda just plodded along ticking off the kilometers before arriving at Mountain Chutes, with a short overgrown ~170 m portage to deal with around the rapid and then back to the car. We were pretty cooked/tired and spent a bit of time swimming and getting semi-organized for the drive back. Loaded up and headed to Temiskaming Shores for Quesada burrito + McDonald's Mcflurry + approximately 1 L of coffee for Mike Patte. Then a 7 hour mission back to Waterloo, home by 2 am.

## Trip Overall: 

~205 km, 66 portages (22 km worth, but really more like 50ish portages and 17 km worth as we skipped a bunch running/walking rapids), 47ish hours of travel time.

[Strava](https://www.strava.com/activities/19680924242)

Overall a great trip. The second day and the last two days were definitely doozies but those "doozies" were 100% voluntary. Day 2 we collectively decided to push on to make for an easier day the next day (having a full day at the base of Maple Mountain is super nice), and days 7 and 8 effectively compressed 3 big days into 2 massive ones, but we all were up for it and weren't ever forced to bite off more than we could chew (i.e. we knew exactly what were biting off). 

There were some easier days offsetting these as well, again it's so so nice to have a whole day at the foot of Maple Mountain, plus if you travel well you can go Maple Mountain to Bridal Veil in ~5 hours, which means having basically an entire afternoon at Bridal Veil (absolute heaven) as well. The S Tier campsite at the south end of Lady Evelyn Lake was awesome too and a great place to hang out after a relatively short day too.

We ate really well and had only ~2 rainy days (which made Shangri La campsite a bit anti-climactic unfortunately, I'm sure I'll be back). We really weren't affected by wind except for the last 17 km of the trip too. Matt's foot cut was the only serious injury and he (+ me and Jasper babying him at the portage put ins/outs) took really good care of it especially the first few days after and it ended up not presenting much issue other than some anxiety about potential infection that never materialized.

Matt remarked after how well the group worked together and noone stood around not doing anything when there were chores to be done. Our camp duties/roles kinda just fell into place. Making camp we'd set up tents/bedding, erect the tarp if rain was possible, gather and split firewood, and assemble camp chair + Matt's (amazing) hammock. Then we'd swim + lounge around reading our books or napping. We'd start dinner 4:30-5 pm usually, which would involve building a fire, prepping food, cooking it, eating it, then doing dishes. While dishes were being done, we'd also dig out the next day's breakfast + lunch so we'd have it all at the top of the barrel first thing the next morning. Then by 6:30-7 we'd have all our chores and eating done, which meant "whiskey oclock", sipping our daily 2 oz ration while we looked out at the view and/or played euchre. By 8:30ish we'd be brushing teeth, packing up and heading to bed. Mornings started around 5:30 (first light) with getting a fire going and boiling ~4 L of water for coffee and then also making whatever breakfast was, while everyone else would pack up bedding/tents. Once breakfast/coffee was done it'd be doing dishes, packing up everything else (tarp/camp chairs etc.), topping up water (thanks Lance), and getting ready for the travel day (brush teeth, sunscreen, packing snacks etc.). On the water paddling usually before 8 am. Really nice efficient schedule, and with the exception of the last two days, lots of mid/late afternoon and early evening time to lounge around.

The portaging setup was a little tricky because of the solo paddler, who sometimes would transport 2 packs in their boat, which meant the third guy in my canoe would need to carry it for them. Other times we just fit that extra pack into our canoe and dealt with it, though it really overloaded our boat even more :-D. Really though, we communicated the carrying logistics quite well and there was literally one "oh we all forgot this pack" moment (and it was 100% my fault), and that involved maybe 100 m of backtracking tops. Solo boat (usually Lance) would take his 90 L pack, or a barrel sometimes, + his canoe. Patte would take a barrel and his canoe, Greg would take his big 115 L pack + lifejackets and paddles. Jasper would take a big canoe pack + daypack and paddles/lifejackets, Matt would take the heaviest barrel + a daypack/paddles/lifejackets. I'd take my canoe + a barrel and/or Lance's 90 L pack.

Our canoes definitely took a beating going down the Makobe and a tougher whitewater boat would have been nice to have then/ but post trip my "kevlar fusion" prospector was really only in need of a fresh epoxy layer + a couple marine gelcoats: a couple hours of maintenance effort and she's ready for the next trip, no worse for wear, actually kind of remarkable when you consider the scrapes she took, often under a very high payload (Matt+Jasper+Me+Packs was well north of 650 lbs, solidly above the recommended load range).
